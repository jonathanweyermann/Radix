# Radix / Beyond the Void: the story

## The Silence

Nobody heard them coming. For one night every radio telescope on Earth picked up the same sound: a chord of eleven layered tones from a dim red star thirty-eight light-years away. Astronomers called it the Choir. People argued about how to answer it.

Nine days later the swarm arrived. Millions of needle-shaped fighters came out of nothing, too many and too fast to count. They did not land, demand anything or answer any message. They burned the cities, cracked the oceans and stripped the atmosphere. Earth went quiet in forty hours. The survivors call it the Silence.

About forty thousand people are left, scattered across places too small or too cold to be worth a second pass: the ice mines of Ceres Deep, the research domes of Ashgrove on Titan, and the old relay station Far Lantern at the edge of the Kuiper belt. They live on rationed air and borrowed time, and they listen for the chord every night.

## The salvage

One Choir fighter did not make it home. It clipped a mining tether over Callisto and buried itself in the ice, nearly intact. Its pilot, a grown thing in a tank of amber fluid, was dead. Its machinery was not.

Dr. Tomas Reyes spent two years taking it apart. The fighter's drive, its shield emitters and, most important, its signature mask went into the last human strike fighter, rebuilt around them. To Choir sensors at a distance, the new ship looks like one of their own returning from a hunt. Up close the disguise fails: anything that actually sees the ship knows it for what it is, and calls the others.

The survivors named the ship **Radix**, Latin for root. Earth was the tree. They are what is left in the ground.

## The Requiem

The heavy cruiser **Requiem** was a deep-space survey ship that happened to be beyond Neptune on the day of the Silence. It is the only human vessel left that can cross the dark between stars. Its crew refitted it with stealth plating and a Choir-derived drive and filled its hold with one fighter, one pilot and every weapon core Reyes could graft onto it.

Captain Sera Halvorsen commands it. Her orders from the outposts were one line long: *Find where they live.*

The trip to the red star, which the crew named **Nadir**, took fourteen months. The Requiem traced the swarm's route back by the scars it left in the interstellar dust. It arrives in silence, hangs in the shadow of an outer moon, and opens its bay doors.

The time for negotiation passed forty hours after the chord.

## The Nadir system

The Requiem's survey names Nadir's worlds with Greek letters, outermost first, and their moons by number. Every one of them is industry: nothing in the system is wasted on beauty.

- **Delta-9**: a relay moon that passes the Choir's song between worlds, and refines poison atmosphere into swarm fuel.
- **Epsilon-5**: the forge moon, where the needle fighters are cast in magma, frozen in cryo vaults until needed, and coordinated by a signal spire.
- **Tau-3**: an ocean moon, home to the shipyards and to the hives where the living pilots are grown.
- **Sigma-4**: terraced refineries that feed the inner worlds.
- **Omega**: the crown world, guarded by the Omega Sentinel.
- **The Void**: inside Omega's orbit, a shroud of dark dust and stolen light hides Nadir's inner system from outside eyes. No signal comes out of it except the chord. That is where the song begins.

## The campaign

### Act I: Landfall (Delta-9)

1. **Relay Station.** The Radix drops onto Delta-9 under the Requiem's shadow. The relay station carries the Choir's signals across the system; cut it, and the strike stays a rumor for a few more hours.
2. **Toxin Refinery.** Delta-9's air is poison, and the Choir drink it. The refinery turns the atmosphere into fuel for the swarm. Every tank that burns is a fighter that never launches.

### Act II: The Forge Moon (Epsilon-5)

3. **Cryo Vaults.** Thousands of finished fighters sleep in the cold, racked and waiting for the next world to be found. The vaults have a well that goes down a long way.
4. **Magma Works.** The foundry where the needles are cast. The lava fields run deeper than the old surveys showed.
5. **Signal Spire.** The spire coordinates every defense on Epsilon-5, and the **Spire Overseer** holds its summit, shielded by two turret relays. Take it down and Epsilon goes deaf.

### Act III: The Brood Sea (Tau-3)

6. **Drowned Docks.** Cargo stacks and harbor water. This is where the swarm is loaded for the long crossing between stars.
7. **Hive Warrens.** The pilots of the swarm are not built; they are grown. In the nest at the bottom of the warrens, the **Hive Queen** lays them, shielded by egg sacs that hatch as you approach. Her death ends the next generation of Earth-burners before it is born.
8. **Tether Anchor.** A space elevator lifts the swarm from Tau-3 to the orbital yards. Cut the anchor and the yards starve.
9. **Orbital Garden.** With the tether cut, the yards have one larder left: a garden cylinder turning over Tau-3, a world rolled up inside a spinning drum. Its meadows, lakes and town curve up round the Radix and meet overhead. Burn the seed towers and the irrigation pumps on the far shore, and the yards go hungry.
10. **Orbital Yards.** The yards the garden fed: a double drum, an industrial cylinder round a second inner one, where the swarm's hulls are pressed and fitted. Wreck the presses, break the bulkhead and kill the dynamos that spin the inner drum.

### Act IV: The Crown (Sigma-4 and Omega)

11. **Refinery Terraces.** The last supply line to the crown world.
12. **Shepherd Moon.** The terraces' last fuel already went up to Sigma-4's shepherd moon, a grey rock two kilometres across that ships it on to Omega. Its depot sits on open ground under a black sky: find the tanker that went down, silence the comms mesa, break into the dome and kill its power cores, then get out.
13. **High Station.** The fuel the moon shipped is stranded at Omega's high station, a fortress of hull and truss in open space over the crown world. Strip its turrets, force the docking port, and burn the reactor slung beneath it.
14. **Rift Lands.** Below the falling station, the only way to Omega's core runs through a plateau split by a rift a hundred units deep.
15. **Core Breach.** The **Omega Sentinel** guards the core that powers Omega's defenses, shielded by four pylons. When it falls, Halvorsen expects the song to stop.

It doesn't. Omega was never the source. The signal threads further in, past the crown world, into the shroud.

### Act V: Beyond the Void

16. **Abyss Shaft.** The first structure inside the Void is a world hollowed out to its core. The Radix descends a shaft over a hundred units deep, past ledges, crystal spires and laser-guarded tunnels, and rides the updraft back out.
17. **Crystal Canyon.** At the end of a canyon of ice and crystal the **Void Carrier** is docked. It is one of the carriers that brought the swarm to Earth, and its hull is still scored from the crossing.
18. **Drift Foundry.** A foundry inside the Void, building something larger than fighters.
19. **The Ark.** The foundry's ship had already left its dock: the Choir's ark, a hull kilometres long, drifting deeper into the Void on its own reactor. The Radix boards through the docking bay, kills the governors that spin the habitat drum and the regulators that hold the reactor steady, and gets off at the bridge before it tears itself apart.
20. **Mirror Archive.** The Choir keep records. Three data cores seal the archive, and destroying them breaks it open. What it shows is the reason the song exists: a catalog of three hundred and twelve silenced worlds. Earth is the latest entry. Every one of them was found the same way: their first radio voices reached Nadir, and the **Herald** heard them, and sang the swarm toward them.
21. **Ash Mesas.** A dead plain under a red sky. This was a living world once, the three hundred and eleventh. The Choir dragged its corpse inside the Void and built the Herald's listening array across it. Three tuning cores keep the array aimed. Destroy them and the Herald goes deaf to everything but you.
22. **Heart of the Void.** At the center of the shroud the **Void Herald** waits: a crown of living crystal, guarded by three orbiting nodes, the voice that told the swarm where Earth was. It sings. Make it stop.

## The ending

When the Herald dies, the song stops. Across the stars, wherever swarms are hunting, the fighters lose their voice and drift. The Requiem's sensors record the Choir's channel going flat for the first time since the night of the chord.

Halvorsen recovers the Radix from the shroud and sends one message home, slow and patient across the dark:

> *It's quiet out here now. Their kind of quiet.*

Then she turns the Requiem toward Sol. There is a root in the ground back there, and roots grow.

## How the game's systems fit the story

- **Stealth signature.** Enemies far away mistake the Radix for one of their own. Anything that sees it raises the alarm, and nearby hostiles wake up. That is why enemies ignore you until they spot you.
- **Warp gates.** The green gates are Choir transit gates. The stolen signature lets the Radix use them to hop between sites.
- **Upgrades.** Amber crystals are Choir weapon cores; Reyes built the Radix to graft them in, which is how the neutron cannon climbs from MK I Pulse to MK VII Lance. Violet crystals are swarm-pod racks for the launcher, pink crystals are seeker munitions, and green crystals are repair nanites from Choir maintenance depots.
- **Checkpoints.** Each opened gate or downed boss lets the Requiem mark a recovery point with a low-power ping.
- **Elites.** Gold-eyed enemies are Earth-burners: veterans of the swarm that destroyed Earth. The Choir mark each silenced world in gold.
- **Escorts.** Wardens flying with two drones are honor guards.
- **Phantoms.** The Void's own stealth hunters. Inside the shroud, the Choir hide even from each other.
- **Data cores.** In the Mirror Archive they hold the catalog of silenced worlds; on the Ash Mesas they tune the Herald's listening array.
- **Reinforced walls.** Choir vaults built to stand up to their own needles. Missiles and plasma were built to answer them.

## In-game text

### Story page (before the menu)

> Thirty-eight light-years away, a red star sang a chord, and nine days later its swarm burned the Earth in forty hours. Forty thousand of us survived on frozen outposts, and from the wreck of one of their fighters we built the Radix, a human ship that wears their signature. The stealth cruiser Requiem carried it fourteen months across the dark to Nadir, the system the swarm came from. Now the Radix drops onto their worlds to burn the refineries, foundries, hives and gates that built the swarm. Somewhere beyond the Void, at the heart of their system, is the voice that called them to Earth, and the time for talking is over.

### Mission briefings (before each sector, under the system map)

1. **DELTA-9 RELAY STATION**: The Requiem crossed fourteen months of dark and now hides in the shadow of an outer moon, unseen. Your first target is the relay station on Delta-9, which passes the Choir's signals between their worlds. Cut it, and they won't know what is hitting them for a while longer.
2. **DELTA-9 TOXIN REFINERY**: The Delta-9 relay is dark: they know something is wrong, but not what. The same moon's refinery turns its poison air into fuel for the swarm. Burn the tanks and vats, because every one is a fighter that never launches.
3. **EPSILON-5 CRYO VAULTS**: Delta-9's refinery is burning, and the fire can be seen from orbit. On Epsilon-5, the forge moon, finished fighters sleep in cryo vaults, waiting for the next world to be found. Destroy the vaults before they wake.
4. **EPSILON-5 MAGMA WORKS**: The cryo vaults are wrecked, and the fighters racked inside them will never fly. Epsilon-5's magma works cast the swarm's hulls from rivers of molten slag. Break the foundry and the swarm stops growing.
5. **EPSILON-5 SIGNAL SPIRE**: The foundry is slag. The last target on Epsilon-5 is the signal spire that runs its defenses, held by the Spire Overseer. Destroy its two relays, then the Overseer, and the forge moon goes deaf and blind.
6. **TAU-3 DROWNED DOCKS**: Epsilon-5 is silent and its forges are cold. Tau-3 is an ocean moon whose docks load the swarm for the crossing between stars. Sink the docks, and watch for gold-marked enemies: they were at Earth.
7. **TAU-3 HIVE WARRENS**: The docks are burning, and no swarm will load there again. Under Tau-3's surface are the hive warrens where the Choir grow their pilots, ruled by the Hive Queen. Burst her egg sacs, then kill her, and the next generation of Earth-burners is never born.
8. **TAU-3 TETHER ANCHOR**: The Hive Queen is dead and her nest is quiet. A tether anchored on Tau-3 lifts finished fighters up to the orbital yards. Cut it and the yards starve.
9. **TAU-3 ORBITAL GARDEN**: The tether is down, but the orbital yards have one more larder: a garden cylinder turning over Tau-3, a world rolled up inside a spinning drum, with meadows, lakes and a town that curve up round you and meet overhead. Destroy its seed towers, fly up to the sun line down its axis, cross to the far shore to wreck the irrigation pumps, and leave by the end-cap gate.
10. **TAU-3 ORBITAL YARDS**: The garden is burning, and the yards it fed are next: a double drum turning over Tau-3, an industrial cylinder round a second inner one, where the swarm's hulls are pressed and fitted. Wreck the press controllers, break the yard bulkhead, climb through a gap into the inner cylinder, kill the spindle dynamos that turn it, and leave by the end-cap gate.
11. **SIGMA-4 REFINERY TERRACES**: The yards are slag and Tau-3 is cut off. Sigma-4's refinery terraces are the last supply line feeding the crown world. Burn them and Omega stands alone.
12. **SIGMA-4B SHEPHERD MOON**: The terraces are burning, but their last fuel already went up the line to Sigma-4's shepherd moon, a grey rock two kilometres across that ships it on to Omega. Its depot sits on open ground under a black sky. Find the tanker that went down, silence the comms mesa, break into the dome and kill its power cores, then get out through the extraction gate.
13. **OMEGA HIGH STATION**: The shepherd moon is dark, and the fuel it shipped is stranded at Omega's high station, a fortress of hull and truss hanging in open space over the crown world. Strip its hull turrets, force the docking port, kill the control cores in its hub, then dive under it and burn the stabilizers round its reactor.
14. **OMEGA RIFT LANDS**: The high station is falling out of orbit. Below it, the only way to the crown world's core runs through the rift lands: a plateau split by a rift a hundred units deep. Destroy the beacons on its rims, dive into the rift, wreck the generators in the bunker on its floor, silence the guns on the east mesa, and follow the rift to its end.
15. **OMEGA CORE BREACH**: The rift is quiet and the way down is open. The crown world's core is guarded by the Omega Sentinel and its four shield pylons. Bring the Sentinel down; this should end the song.
16. **VOID-1 ABYSS SHAFT**: The Sentinel is dead, but the song did not stop: Omega was never the source. The signal comes from inside the Void, a shroud of dark dust around Nadir's inner worlds, and the Requiem is going in. Follow it down the Abyss Shaft, a world hollowed out to its core.
17. **VOID-2 CRYSTAL CANYON**: You made it back up the shaft, deeper inside the Void than anyone has been. At the end of a crystal canyon a Void Carrier is docked, one of the ships that carried the swarm to Earth. Take it apart.
18. **VOID-3 DRIFT FOUNDRY**: The carrier that burned Earth is wreckage. Deeper in, a foundry is building something far bigger than fighters. Climb it and tear it down before it is finished.
19. **THE ARK REACTOR SPINE**: The foundry is down, but the ship it was building had already slipped its dock: the Choir's ark, a hull kilometres long, drifting deeper into the Void on its own reactor. Board it through the docking bay, kill the governors that spin its habitat drum, then the regulators that hold its reactor steady, and get off at the bridge before it tears itself apart.
20. **VOID-4 MIRROR ARCHIVE**: The ark is dead and drifting, and whatever it was built to carry will never arrive. The Mirror Archive holds the Choir's records behind three data cores. Destroy the cores and find out what the song is for.
21. **VOID-5 ASH MESAS**: The archive listed 312 silenced worlds, and Earth was the latest; every one was found when the Herald heard its first radio voices. The ash plain ahead is world 311, and the Herald's listening array stands on it, aimed by three data cores. Destroy them and the Herald goes deaf to everything but you.
22. **VOID-6 HEART OF THE VOID**: The array is blind: the Herald can only hear you now. It waits at the heart of the Void, shielded by three orbiting nodes. Silence it, and the swarm loses its voice everywhere.
