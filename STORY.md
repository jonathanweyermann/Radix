# Radix / Beyond the Void: the story

## The Silence

Nobody heard them coming. For one night every radio telescope on Earth picked up the same sound: a chord of eleven layered tones from a dim red star thirty-eight light-years away. Astronomers called it the Choir. People argued about how to answer it.

Nine days later the swarm arrived. Millions of needle-shaped fighters came out of nothing, too many and too fast to count. They did not land, demand anything or answer any message. They burned the cities, cracked the oceans and stripped the atmosphere. Earth went quiet in forty hours. The survivors call it the Silence.

About forty thousand people are left, scattered across places too small or too cold to be worth a second pass: the ice mines of Ceres Deep, the research domes of Ashgrove on Titan, and the old relay station Far Lantern at the edge of the Kuiper belt. They live on rationed air and borrowed time, and they listen for the chord every night.

## The salvage

One Choir fighter did not make it home. It clipped a mining tether over Callisto and buried itself in the ice, nearly intact. Its pilot, a grown thing in a tank of amber fluid, was dead. Its machinery was not.

Dr. Tomas Reyes spent two years taking it apart. The fighter's drive, its shield emitters and, most important, its signature mask went into the last human strike fighter, rebuilt around them. To Choir sensors at a distance, the new ship looks like one of their own returning from a hunt. Up close the disguise fails: anything that actually sees the ship knows it for what it is, and calls the others.

The survivors named the ship **Radix**, Latin for root. Earth was the tree. They are what is left in the ground.

## The Requiem

The heavy cruiser **Requiem** was a deep-space survey ship that happened to be beyond Neptune on the day of the Silence. It is the only human vessel left that can cross the dark between stars. Its crew refitted it with stealth plating and a Choir-derived drive and filled its hold with one fighter, one pilot and every weapon core Reyes could graft onto it.

Captain Sera Halvorsen commands it. Her orders from the outposts were one line long: *Find where they live.*

The trip to the red star, which the crew named **Nadir**, took fourteen months. The Requiem traced the swarm's route back by the scars it left in the interstellar dust. It arrives in silence, hangs in the shadow of an outer moon, and opens its bay doors.

The time for negotiation passed forty hours after the chord.

## The Nadir system

The Requiem's survey names Nadir's worlds with Greek letters, outermost first, and their moons by number. Every one of them is industry: nothing in the system is wasted on beauty.

- **Delta-9**: a relay moon that passes the Choir's song between worlds, and refines poison atmosphere into swarm fuel.
- **Epsilon-5**: the forge moon, where the needle fighters are cast in magma, frozen in cryo vaults until needed, and coordinated by a signal spire.
- **Tau-3**: an ocean moon, home to the shipyards and to the hives where the living pilots are grown.
- **Sigma-4**: terraced refineries that feed the inner worlds.
- **Omega**: the crown world, guarded by the Omega Sentinel.
- **The Void**: inside Omega's orbit, a shroud of dark dust and stolen light hides Nadir's inner system from outside eyes. No signal comes out of it except the chord. That is where the song begins. Inside it, a second system of worlds circles close to the star: Void-1 to Void-6, with the Choir's ark drifting among them.

## The campaign

The campaign is thirty-six sectors in three episodes of twelve. Each episode has its own map in the briefings and ends on a boss.

### Episode I: Landfall

The outer worlds, where the swarm is fuelled, cast, frozen and grown. It ends under Tau-3, in the Hive Queen's nest.

#### Delta-9

1. **Relay Station.** The Radix drops onto Delta-9 under the Requiem's shadow. The relay station carries the Choir's signals across the system; cut it, and the strike stays a rumor for a few more hours.
2. **Toxin Refinery.** Delta-9's air is poison, and the Choir drink it. The refinery turns the atmosphere into fuel for the swarm. Every tank that burns is a fighter that never launches.
3. **Launch Silos.** The refinery's fuel ran north along a pipeline to Delta-9's launch silos, where finished fighters are fuelled and fired into orbit. Kill the pumps, then go down the silos after the fighters racked at the bottom.

#### Epsilon-5, the forge moon

4. **Cryo Vaults.** Thousands of finished fighters sleep in the cold, racked and waiting for the next world to be found. The vaults have a well that goes down a long way.
5. **Glacier Moon.** The cold that kept the vaults came from Epsilon-5's glacier moon, a frozen world of crystal spires with ice caves under its glaciers and a research base studying its core.
6. **Magma Works.** The foundry where the needles are cast. The lava fields run deeper than the old surveys showed.
7. **Magma Gorge.** The foundry's fire runs up from the moon's core along a gorge a hundred units deep, a river of lava between smelters and gun pads on the rims.
8. **Signal Spire.** The spire coordinates every defense on Epsilon-5, and the **Spire Overseer** holds its summit, shielded by two turret relays. Take it down and Epsilon goes deaf.

#### Tau-3, the brood sea

9. **Storm Reef.** Tau-3 watches its sky from a reef of storm-beaten islands strung with listening masts. The Requiem cannot slip into the ocean moon's shadow while they listen.
10. **Drowned Docks.** Cargo stacks and harbor water. This is where the swarm is loaded for the long crossing between stars.
11. **Tide Drum.** Tau-3 farms its tides in orbit too: a drum of sea with islands and harbour towns that curve up and meet overhead.
12. **Hive Warrens.** The pilots of the swarm are not built; they are grown. In the nest at the bottom of the warrens, the **Hive Queen** lays them, shielded by egg sacs that hatch as you approach. Her death ends the next generation of Earth-burners before it is born.

### Episode II: The Crown

Up the gravity well from Tau-3 and in toward Omega, the crown world, through every world that feeds it. It ends at Omega's core, with the Sentinel.

#### Tau-3's orbit

13. **Brood Ark.** The queen's last brood had already launched, in a living ark with a brood mother as big as the queen in its hold.
14. **Tether Anchor.** A space elevator lifts the swarm from Tau-3 to the orbital yards. Cut the anchor and the yards starve.
15. **Orbital Garden.** With the tether cut, the yards have one larder left: a garden cylinder turning over Tau-3, a world rolled up inside a spinning drum. Its meadows, lakes and town curve up round the Radix and meet overhead. Burn the seed towers and the irrigation pumps on the far shore, and the yards go hungry.
16. **Orbital Yards.** The yards the garden fed: a double drum, an industrial cylinder round a second inner one, where the swarm's hulls are pressed and fitted. Wreck the presses, break the bulkhead and kill the dynamos that spin the inner drum.

#### Sigma-4

17. **Dune Wells.** Sigma-4's terraces refine crude drawn from wells sunk through its dunes. Cap the wells and the last supply line to the crown world runs dry at its source.
18. **Refinery Terraces.** The crude the wells sent up is refined on terraces cut into a hill: the last supply line to the crown world.
19. **Shepherd Moon.** The terraces' last fuel already went up to Sigma-4's shepherd moon, a grey rock two kilometres across that ships it on to Omega. Its depot sits on open ground under a black sky: find the tanker that went down, silence the comms mesa, break into the dome and kill its power cores, then get out.
20. **Ore Belt.** Between the moon and Omega, the crown world's hulls are mined from a belt of asteroids round a refinery.

#### Omega

21. **High Station.** The fuel the moon shipped is stranded at Omega's high station, a fortress of hull and truss in open space over the crown world. Strip its turrets, force the docking port, and burn the reactor slung beneath it.
22. **Rift Lands.** Below the falling station, the only way to Omega's core runs through a plateau split by a rift a hundred units deep.
23. **Crown City.** The rift ends under the walls of the crown city, the one place in Nadir the Choir built to dwell in. Its emitter towers hold the shield over the core.
24. **Core Breach.** The **Omega Sentinel** guards the core that powers Omega's defenses, shielded by four pylons. When it falls, Halvorsen expects the song to stop.

It doesn't. Omega was never the source. The signal threads further in, past the crown world, into the shroud.

### Episode III: Beyond the Void

Inside the shroud, where Nadir hides a second system of worlds, to the voice at its heart.

#### The shroud and the hidden worlds

25. **Dust Veil.** The Void's edge is a wall of dust strung with veil buoys and seeded with mines. The Radix goes first, so the Requiem can follow it in unseen.
26. **Abyss Shaft.** The first structure inside the Void is a world hollowed out to its core. The Radix descends a shaft over a hundred units deep, past ledges, crystal spires and laser-guarded tunnels, and rides the updraft back out.
27. **Sunless Sea.** The shaft's updraft throws the Radix into a cavern under the Void's crust: a sea that has never seen a sun, with a drowned shrine on an island in the middle.
28. **Crystal Canyon.** At the end of a canyon of ice and crystal the **Void Carrier** is docked. It is one of the carriers that brought the swarm to Earth, and its hull is still scored from the crossing.
29. **Suncatcher.** The shroud lets no light out because the Choir catch it. Void-2B is a moon tiled with mirrors that turn Nadir's light into power for the foundries deeper in.
30. **Drift Foundry.** A foundry inside the Void, building something larger than fighters.
31. **The Ark.** The foundry's ship had already left its dock: the Choir's ark, a hull kilometres long, drifting deeper into the Void on its own reactor. The Radix boards through the docking bay, kills the governors that spin the habitat drum and the regulators that hold the reactor steady, and gets off at the bridge before it tears itself apart.
32. **Trophy Field.** Past the dead ark the Void opens on rows of wrecks, hulls of every shape, none of them Choir-built. Whoever made them is gone. The archive at the heart of the field will say why.
33. **Mirror Archive.** The Choir keep records. Three data cores seal the archive, and destroying them breaks it open. What it shows is the reason the song exists: a catalog of three hundred and twelve silenced worlds. Earth is the latest entry. Every one of them was found the same way: their first radio voices reached Nadir, and the **Herald** heard them, and sang the swarm toward them.
34. **Ash Mesas.** A dead plain under a red sky. This was a living world once, the three hundred and eleventh. The Choir dragged its corpse inside the Void and built the Herald's listening array across it. Three tuning cores keep the array aimed. Destroy them and the Herald goes deaf to everything but you.
35. **Choir Spires.** Eleven spires ring the heart of the Void, and each sings one tone of the chord Earth heard the night before the swarm came. Silence them, and the Herald sings alone.
36. **Heart of the Void.** At the center of the shroud the **Void Herald** waits: a crown of living crystal, guarded by three orbiting nodes, the voice that told the swarm where Earth was. It sings. Make it stop.

## The ending

When the Herald dies, the song stops. Across the stars, wherever swarms are hunting, the fighters lose their voice and drift. The Requiem's sensors record the Choir's channel going flat for the first time since the night of the chord.

Halvorsen recovers the Radix from the shroud and sends one message home, slow and patient across the dark:

> *It's quiet out here now. Their kind of quiet.*

Then she turns the Requiem toward Sol. There is a root in the ground back there, and roots grow.

## How the game's systems fit the story

- **Stealth signature.** Enemies far away mistake the Radix for one of their own. Anything that sees it raises the alarm, and nearby hostiles wake up. That is why enemies ignore you until they spot you.
- **Warp gates.** The green gates are Choir transit gates. The stolen signature lets the Radix use them to hop between sites.
- **Upgrades.** Amber crystals are Choir weapon cores; Reyes built the Radix to graft them in, which is how the neutron cannon climbs from MK I Pulse to MK VII Lance. Violet crystals are swarm-pod racks for the launcher, pink crystals are seeker munitions, and green crystals are repair nanites from Choir maintenance depots.
- **Checkpoints.** Each opened gate or downed boss lets the Requiem mark a recovery point with a low-power ping.
- **Elites.** Gold-eyed enemies are Earth-burners: veterans of the swarm that destroyed Earth. The Choir mark each silenced world in gold.
- **Escorts.** Wardens flying with two drones are honor guards.
- **Phantoms.** The Void's own stealth hunters. Inside the shroud, the Choir hide even from each other.
- **Data cores.** In the Mirror Archive they hold the catalog of silenced worlds; on the Ash Mesas they tune the Herald's listening array.
- **Reinforced walls.** Choir vaults built to stand up to their own needles. Missiles and plasma were built to answer them.

## In-game text

### Story page (before the menu)

> Thirty-eight light-years away, a red star sang a chord, and nine days later its swarm burned the Earth in forty hours. Forty thousand of us survived on frozen outposts, and from the wreck of one of their fighters we built the Radix, a human ship that wears their signature. The stealth cruiser Requiem carried it fourteen months across the dark to Nadir, the system the swarm came from. Now the Radix drops onto their worlds to burn the refineries, foundries, hives and gates that built the swarm. Somewhere beyond the Void, at the heart of their system, is the voice that called them to Earth, and the time for talking is over.

### Mission briefings (before each sector, under the episode map)

**EPISODE I // LANDFALL**

1. **DELTA-9 RELAY STATION**: The Requiem crossed fourteen months of dark and now hides in the shadow of an outer moon, unseen. Your first target is the relay station on Delta-9, which passes the Choir's signals between their worlds. Cut it, and they won't know what is hitting them for a while longer.
2. **DELTA-9 TOXIN REFINERY**: The Delta-9 relay is dark: they know something is wrong, but not what. The same moon's refinery turns its poison air into fuel for the swarm. Burn the tanks and vats, because every one is a fighter that never launches.
3. **DELTA-9 LAUNCH SILOS**: The refinery is burning, but the fuel it made already ran north along the pipeline to Delta-9's launch silos, where finished fighters are fuelled and fired into orbit. Destroy the fuel pumps along the pipeline, dive into the silos to kill the fighters racked at the bottom, then break into the launch bunker and wreck its control core.
4. **EPSILON-5 CRYO VAULTS**: Delta-9's silos are smoking holes, and the fire can be seen from orbit. On Epsilon-5, the forge moon, finished fighters sleep in cryo vaults, waiting for the next world to be found. Destroy the vaults before they wake.
5. **EPSILON-5B GLACIER MOON**: The cryo vaults are wrecked, but the cold that kept them came from Epsilon-5's glacier moon, a frozen world of crystal spires and buried ice. Shatter the cryo pylons in the spire fields, go down into the ice caves to wreck the thaw pumps, then break into the research base and destroy its core samples.
6. **EPSILON-5 MAGMA WORKS**: The glacier moon is dark, and no vault will be cooled again. Epsilon-5's magma works cast the swarm's hulls from rivers of molten slag. Break the foundry and the swarm stops growing.
7. **EPSILON-5 MAGMA GORGE**: The foundry is slag, but its fire still runs: a gorge a hundred units deep feeds the furnaces with lava from the moon's core. Destroy the smelter valves on its rims, dive into the gorge to wreck the pumps standing in the lava, silence the foundry guns, and fly out through the gate at the gorge's end.
8. **EPSILON-5 SIGNAL SPIRE**: The gorge has run cold. The last target on Epsilon-5 is the signal spire that runs its defenses, held by the Spire Overseer. Destroy its two relays, then the Overseer, and the forge moon goes deaf and blind.
9. **TAU-3 STORM REEF**: Epsilon-5 is deaf, but the Choir know now that something is loose among their worlds. Tau-3, the ocean moon, watches its sky from a reef of islands strung with listening masts, and the Requiem cannot slip into its shadow while they listen. Destroy the masts, sink the picket ships that patrol the lagoon, and leave through the storm gate.
10. **TAU-3 DROWNED DOCKS**: The reef has gone quiet and the Requiem is in Tau-3's shadow, unseen. The ocean moon's docks load the swarm for the crossing between stars. Sink the docks, and watch for gold-marked enemies: they were at Earth.
11. **TAU-3 TIDE DRUM**: The docks are burning, but Tau-3 farms its tides too: a drum of sea turns in orbit over the moon, its islands and harbours curving up round you to meet overhead. Destroy the harbour cranes, open the sea gate in the wall that rings the drum, cross to the far shore and destroy the desalination cores.
12. **TAU-3 HIVE WARRENS**: The tide drum is drained, and no swarm will load or feed there again. Under Tau-3's surface are the hive warrens where the Choir grow their pilots, ruled by the Hive Queen. Burst her egg sacs, then kill her, and the next generation of Earth-burners is never born.

**EPISODE II // THE CROWN**

13. **TAU-3 BROOD ARK**: The Hive Queen is dead, but her last brood had already launched: a living ark drifting out of Tau-3's orbit, its hold full of eggs round a brood mother as big as the queen was. Destroy the spore turrets on its hide, shoot open the maw, burst the egg sacs and kill the brood mother.
14. **TAU-3 TETHER ANCHOR**: The brood ark is dead and Tau-3's nests are quiet. A tether anchored on Tau-3 lifts finished fighters up to the orbital yards. Cut it and the yards starve.
15. **TAU-3 ORBITAL GARDEN**: The tether is down, but the orbital yards have one more larder: a garden cylinder turning over Tau-3, a world rolled up inside a spinning drum, with meadows, lakes and a town that curve up round you and meet overhead. Destroy its seed towers, fly up to the sun line down its axis, cross to the far shore to wreck the irrigation pumps, and leave by the end-cap gate.
16. **TAU-3 ORBITAL YARDS**: The garden is burning, and the yards it fed are next: a double drum turning over Tau-3, an industrial cylinder round a second inner one, where the swarm's hulls are pressed and fitted. Wreck the press controllers, break the yard bulkhead, climb through a gap into the inner cylinder, kill the spindle dynamos that turn it, and leave by the end-cap gate.
17. **SIGMA-4 DUNE WELLS**: The yards are slag and Tau-3 is cut off. The Requiem moves in on Sigma-4, whose refinery terraces are the last supply line to the crown world, and they draw their crude from wells sunk through the dunes below. Cap the wellheads, kill the sand crawler hauling the last load north, and follow the pipeline out of the dunes.
18. **SIGMA-4 REFINERY TERRACES**: The wells are burning and the pipeline has run dry. What crude reached the refinery terraces before it did is all Sigma-4 has left to send the crown world. Burn the terraces and Omega stands alone.
19. **SIGMA-4B SHEPHERD MOON**: The terraces are burning, but their last fuel already went up the line to Sigma-4's shepherd moon, a grey rock two kilometres across that ships it on to Omega. Its depot sits on open ground under a black sky. Find the tanker that went down, silence the comms mesa, break into the dome and kill its power cores, then get out through the extraction gate.
20. **SIGMA-4 ORE BELT**: The shepherd moon is dark. Between it and Omega runs an ore belt where the crown world's hulls are mined: asteroids bored by drill rigs, strung together by conveyor trusses round a refinery. Destroy the drill rigs, force the refinery door and wreck its smelter cores, then hunt down the ore tug before it reaches Omega.
21. **OMEGA HIGH STATION**: The ore belt is wreckage, and the fuel the moon shipped is stranded at Omega's high station, a fortress of hull and truss hanging in open space over the crown world. Strip its hull turrets, force the docking port, kill the control cores in its hub, then dive under it and burn the stabilizers round its reactor.
22. **OMEGA RIFT LANDS**: The high station is falling out of orbit. Below it, the only way to the crown world's core runs through the rift lands: a plateau split by a rift a hundred units deep. Destroy the beacons on its rims, dive into the rift, wreck the generators in the bunker on its floor, silence the guns on the east mesa, and follow the rift to its end.
23. **OMEGA CROWN CITY**: The rift ends under the walls of the crown city, the one place in Nadir the Choir built to dwell in rather than to make war from. Three emitter towers stand over it and hold the shield that covers the core. Break the emitters on the tower tops, force the citadel gate, kill the core locks inside, and take the shaft down.
24. **OMEGA CORE BREACH**: The crown city's shield is down and the shaft to the core is open. The core is guarded by the Omega Sentinel and its four shield pylons. Bring the Sentinel down; this should end the song.

**EPISODE III // BEYOND THE VOID**

25. **THE SHROUD DUST VEIL**: The Sentinel is dead, but the song did not stop: Omega was never the source. The signal comes from inside the Void, a shroud of dark dust around Nadir's inner worlds, and the Requiem is going in. The shroud's edge is strung with veil buoys that would see it coming. Destroy the buoys, kill the minelayers seeding the passage, and lead the Requiem through.
26. **VOID-1 ABYSS SHAFT**: The Requiem is through the veil, deeper in than any survey has seen. The first world inside the Void has been hollowed out to its core, and the signal runs straight down through it. Follow it down the Abyss Shaft.
27. **VOID-1B SUNLESS SEA**: The shaft's updraft threw you out sideways, into a cavern under the Void's crust: a sea that has never seen a sun, under a roof hung with stalactites. Douse the void lanterns glowing on its shores, cross the water to the drowned shrine on its island, and silence the echo cores inside.
28. **VOID-2 CRYSTAL CANYON**: You crossed the sunless sea and came up deeper inside the Void than anyone has been. At the end of a crystal canyon a Void Carrier is docked, one of the ships that carried the swarm to Earth. Take it apart.
29. **VOID-2B SUNCATCHER**: The carrier that burned Earth is wreckage. The shroud lets no light out because the Choir catch it: Void-2B is a moon tiled with mirrors, turned to Nadir, beaming stolen sunlight to the foundries deeper in. Shatter the focus lenses across its face, then destroy the emitter in the beam tower, and the foundries go dark.
30. **VOID-3 DRIFT FOUNDRY**: The suncatcher is dark, and the foundry it lit is running on reserves. It is building something far bigger than fighters. Climb it and tear it down before it is finished.
31. **THE ARK REACTOR SPINE**: The foundry is down, but the ship it was building had already slipped its dock: the Choir's ark, a hull kilometres long, drifting deeper into the Void on its own reactor. Board it through the docking bay, kill the governors that spin its habitat drum, then the regulators that hold its reactor steady, and get off at the bridge before it tears itself apart.
32. **VOID-4 TROPHY FIELD**: The ark is dead and drifting. Beyond it the Void opens on a field of wrecks hung in rows: hulls of every shape and none of them built by the Choir. Whoever made them is gone. Destroy the ward beacons that guard the field, force the dock on the great wreck at its heart, and fly on to the archive.
33. **VOID-4 MIRROR ARCHIVE**: The wrecks were ships from other worlds, and the archive at the heart of the field keeps the Choir's records behind three data cores. Destroy the cores and find out what the song is for.
34. **VOID-5 ASH MESAS**: The archive listed 312 silenced worlds, and Earth was the latest; every one was found when the Herald heard its first radio voices. The ash plain ahead is world 311, and the Herald's listening array stands on it, aimed by three data cores. Destroy them and the Herald goes deaf to everything but you.
35. **VOID-6 CHOIR SPIRES**: The array is blind, and the Herald can hear only you. Round the heart of the Void stand the choir spires, eleven of them, and each sings one tone of the chord Earth heard the night before the swarm came. Silence all eleven, and the Herald will sing alone.
36. **VOID-6 HEART OF THE VOID**: The choir is silent and the Herald sings alone. It waits at the heart of the Void, shielded by three orbiting nodes. Silence it, and the swarm loses its voice everywhere.
