# Radix / Beyond the Void

A tiny dependency-free 3D space-flight prototype drawn with terminal characters.

## Run in a browser

Open `index.html` in a browser that supports WebGL 2. No installation or web server is needed. The game opens on a short story page (the menu theme starts once you tap or press a key), then a menu where you can continue, start a new game, pick a difficulty, choose any sector you have reached, and see the arsenal tier list. Starting a sector, from the menu or through a warp gate, first shows a mission briefing: a map of the episode's part of the Nadir system (the outer worlds, the crown worlds round Omega, or the hidden worlds inside the Void) with the route from the last target to the next and the targets cleared so far this episode, and a few sentences on what the last sector achieved and what this one has to. Press LAUNCH or Enter to fly. The full story is in [STORY.md](STORY.md). Click the game to capture the mouse; Esc releases it and opens the menu, which pauses the game.

**Phones and tablets:** play it at https://jonathanweyermann.github.io/Radix/ with the device held sideways. On touch screens the game shows on-screen controls and a smaller cockpit. Touch anywhere on the left half of the screen and drag to steer; the stick appears under your thumb and reads the direction from where you first touched. Push up to climb, and the nose eases back to level when you let go. The PITCH button in the menu flips this to the mouse's inverted style. Hold THRUST or BRAKE to change speed, which stays where you leave it. Hold FIRE to shoot, and tap MSL to launch a missile. MAP and MENU sit at the top right, and the menu has a FULL SCREEN button. Strafing is keyboard only. Switching apps pauses the game. Add `?touch` to the address to force the touch controls on, `?desktop` to turn them off, or `?debugtouch` to show the raw touch coordinates on screen.

**Graphics:** the menu has HIGH, MEDIUM and LOW settings and shows your frame rate in flight. HIGH renders at up to twice the screen's pixel density with 16 dynamic lights; MEDIUM at the screen's own resolution with 8. Both draw the scene into a high-range buffer and finish it with one tone curve, a light bloom and a vignette; LOW at 60% resolution with 4 lights, flat lighting without bump mapping or highlights, no clouds and fewer glows, which is about three to four times faster on weak graphics chips. LOW also turns off antialiasing once the page is reloaded. If the frame rate stays under 24 after a few seconds of flight, the game suggests LOW.

**Difficulty:** Easy spawns about two thirds as many enemies, lets 60% of incoming damage through, and enemies aim where you are. Normal enemies aim partly ahead of a moving ship. Hard spawns 30% more enemies, deals 35% more damage, and enemies lead their shots almost fully. Changing it mid-flight changes damage and aim at once, and enemy numbers from the next sector you start.

**Progress** is saved in the browser (localStorage) as you enter each sector: the last sector you flew, your difficulty, the cannon, launcher and missiles you last entered each sector with, and the best cannon and launcher you have ever reached (shown in the menu's tier list). Picking a sector from the menu starts it with that loadout and a full hull. Move the mouse to steer, with up/down inverted. Hold the left mouse button to fire the cannon, and press C to launch seeker missiles. Hold the right mouse button for full thrust; when you let go the ship drops back to the speed it had before. The weapons make short synthesized sounds.

The keyboard controls are W/A/S/D to strafe up, left, down, and right, Up to pitch down, Down to pitch up, Left/Right to turn, Q/E to roll, X to accelerate, Z to brake, F/J to fire, C to launch a missile, Tab to toggle the map, P to pause, and M to toggle music. Keys 1 to 5 fire any active powers you have bought (see Upgrades). Space also accelerates. Shoot hostile ships and the explosive barrels on the floor; barrel blasts also damage nearby enemies. The sky is visible, but a flight boundary prevents escape above the walls. Solid walls block your view and shots. The cockpit shows radar, shield, hull, speed, heading, altitude, sector, cannon tier, missile count (with the salvo size once the launcher is upgraded), target counts, and the name of the current area. A blue shield soaks up the first few hits before the hull takes damage; it holds about a third of a full hull, starts recharging four seconds after the last hit, and refills at 7% a second. Every hit shakes the view, harder for bigger hits. Hits that only reach the shield flash the screen edge blue with a crackling force-field sound; hits that reach the hull land with a heavy metal crunch, shake the view harder and wash the screen red. If the hull reaches zero you are destroyed. Opening any gate or destroying a boss saves a checkpoint, so press R (or tap FIRE on a touch screen) to restart from the last one: the enemies you destroyed stay destroyed, used barrels and pickups stay gone, opened gates stay open, and you get back the loadout you had then with at least 60% hull. Shift+R restarts the sector from its start with the loadout you entered it with.

### Sectors

There are thirty-six sectors in three episodes of twelve, and each episode ends on a boss. Each sector ends at a green warp gate; fly through it to move on to the next sector. The radar always shows the gate's bearing as a blinking green dot, pinned to the rim when the gate is out of range. Each warp repairs 25% of the hull and refills the shield, and your cannon, launcher and missiles carry over. Each sector has its own music, look and sky, with its own banded planet hanging over it, some open with a short objective, and enemies get a little tougher in every later sector, up to about two and a third times as tough in the last. Each warp shows a results line with the hostiles you destroyed, the secrets you found (hidden panels and reinforced walls) and your time, and the menu keeps your best time for each sector. After the thirty-sixth sector the game shows your run: the sector you started from, the total flight time and the hostiles destroyed. Press R to fly again from the start.

#### Episode I: Landfall

Delta-9, Epsilon-5 and the surface of Tau-3, ending on the Hive Queen.

1. **DELTA-9 Relay Station**: a dark, enclosed computer station with walls of consoles. From the entry hall, a chained gate opens into a domed control hub. Routes then run east across a round cooling pond on a narrow walkway, through a computer annex and coolant hall, or west through a yard stacked with cargo containers and a hidden armory. Both routes lead to a long gallery, and a last chained gate opens onto an exit approach crossed by a slow laser grid and the uplink court. From there a covered stair climbs to the antenna field, an open plain around a glowing mast with raised platforms, girders at two heights and a reinforced wall hiding a relay vault. A second route reaches the field from the coolant hall, through a chained coolant tunnel with a laser grid and a transformer yard with an updraft vent. A chained stair at the far end rises to the uplink dish and the warp gate. Its objectives: shut down the relay control console on the hub's pad, destroy the three mast relays standing on the antenna field and its platforms, shoot the locks on the dish stair, destroy the uplink emitter, a horn at the bottom of the dish, then reach the gate on the dish.
2. **DELTA-9 Toxin Refinery**: a rusted refinery of round tanks and pools joined by ducts. Walkways cross a round settling basin, and chained gates lead east to a filter room and a tank farm of domed storage tanks, and north, through a duct with a laser grid, to the barrel-roofed refinery hall with its sludge vats. A lancer snipes from the tank yard to the west, which has its own chained gate into the hall. Two secret panels hide supply caches, and a final gate opens onto the outflow pool and its warp gate. Pipes rise and run along the filter room's walls. Its objectives: burn the two fuel vats, tanks standing in the rimmed sludge pools of the refinery hall that keep burning once hit, destroy the two scrubber drums on the shelves by the scrubber towers, where intake fans turn round the stacks, then ride the flare stack, ribbed with rusted hoops and glowing at its lip, up to the gate.
3. **DELTA-9 Launch Silos**: an open plain under a poison-green sky, walled east and west by ridges, with sludge pools in its hollows. A pipeline lies on a truss on stilts, running north from the refinery past three pump pads, each fed by a branch pipe, a tank farm of domed tanks stands beside it, and four silos are sunk seventy units into the ground, each ringed by a concrete collar with a gantry tower beside it and an updraft vent in its floor to lift you back out. Its objectives: destroy the three fuel pumps, dive into each silo and destroy the fighter racked nose up in its cradle at the bottom beside the launch rails, shoot the locks off the launch bunker, destroy the launch control console among the console banks inside, then reach the launch gate under its gantry. A reinforced wall seals an ammunition shed.
4. **EPSILON-5 Cryo Vaults**: frozen, mostly roofed vaults with ice walls and floors. A chained corridor opens into a barrel-vaulted hall of glowing crystal pillars, with stairs climbing to an upper gallery over a round cold well that drops far below the rest of the station. Another chained gate leads into a chamber of cryo pods guarded by an aegis. A side route runs through a west cold store with a secret frost cache, and the exit vault holds the twin-seeker launcher. Racks of inert fighters line both sides of the vault hall, and banks of cryo pods ring the cryo chamber. Its objectives: destroy the four sleeping fighters waking in the vault hall's racks, shoot the locks on the cryo chamber, smash its three occupied cryo pods, then climb the cryo spire to the gate.
5. **EPSILON-5B Glacier Moon**: a frozen moon twice the usual size, curving away to a close horizon. Crystal spire fields stand among frozen lakes, craters and ice boulders, a high glacier ridge has ice caves carved under it, reached by cuttings in its flanks, and a walled research base stands to the west. Its objectives: shatter four cryo pylons on the tallest spires, enter the ice caves and destroy three coolant pumps, break into the research base, destroy its three core samples, each in a rack beside an instrument console, then reach the launch gate.
6. **EPSILON-5 Magma Works**: an open-air foundry of basalt over molten slag. Causeways cross a lava field with a wandering shore, deep below them, with updraft vents in the pit that lift a falling ship back out. Three chained flues, round bores through the rock, lead into a smelter yard with a great crucible sunk in it, where a miner lays mines. A secret panel in the east flue hides a cache, and a chained duct off it leads to a cooling tower whose updraft lifts you to a high run, past a laser grid, that rejoins the upper works and skips the smelter yard. Stairs climb to the upper works, and a chained vent shaft leads up to the exit deck. Its objectives: destroy the two smelter crucibles in the smelter yard, which burn once broken, smash the three casting moulds in the casting pit, then reach the gate on the crown forge.
7. **EPSILON-5 Magma Gorge**: a slag plateau twice the usual size cut by a winding gorge more than a hundred units deep, with smooth basalt walls and a river of lava along its floor that burns if you fly low over it. Smelters, gun pads and molten vats stand on the rims, and girder bridges cross the gorge. Its objectives: destroy four smelter valves on the rims, dive into the gorge, destroy three magma pumps standing in the lava, silence four foundry guns, then reach the gate down in the gorge.
8. **EPSILON-5 Signal Spire**: a night-time climb in clean white tech panels under a far taller sky. The spire stands in an open plaza watched by lancers, a tapering stack of lit stages with turning collars that climbs out of sight. A dish array and a domed observatory lie to the east. Stairs rise to terraces split by a coolant moat, then to an upper terrace with an aegis. A chained tower bore with a laser grid and a chained west bore lead into the summit hall, where the Spire Overseer bars the long stair to the raised summit deck. Its objectives: destroy the two turret relay masts, then the Spire Overseer, then climb to the gate on the antenna crown, where two lit antenna masts stand on the crown beam.

Its objectives: destroy the four listening masts on the big islands' summits, sink the three picket ships riding the lagoon (gunboats that list and go under when hit hard enough), then reach the storm gate on its raft. A reinforced wall in the central islet hides the reef cache.

A side route runs through a submarine pen under an arched roof, and the storm seawall curves across the outer harbour to the lighthouse. 11. **TAU-3 Tide Drum**: a cylinder of sea twice the garden's size, its wooded islands, pilot roosts, piers, cranes and lighthouses curving up round you to meet overhead. A sea wall rings the whole drum with one chained gate, and a desalination plant stands on the far shore, across the axis. Its objectives: destroy the hoists of four harbour cranes, open the sea gate, cross to the far shore, destroy three desalination cores, each between two filter drums, then reach the end-cap gate.
12. **TAU-3 Hive Warrens**: a warren that was grown, not built: rounded chambers under resin domes, joined by tubes that wander as they run. Broods hatch mites in the brood chamber and the egg vault. Both routes from the brood chamber end at chained gates into the nest, a great domed chamber around a resin pillar where the Hive Queen waits, and the stair beyond stays sealed until she dies. The stair climbs to the upper warren, whose secret royal cache holds the swarm pod, and a breach tunnel opens to the sky and the warp gate. Its objectives: burst the four egg sacs in the nest, kill the Hive Queen, then break out up the tunnel behind her and through the crown nest.

#### Episode II: The Crown

Tau-3's orbit, Sigma-4 and Omega, ending on the OMEGA Sentinel.

13. **TAU-3 Brood Ark**: a living ship in open space, its rounded hive hull grown round a hold, with two smaller pods and egg spheres drifting round it. You enter through the maw into a ribbed gullet that narrows through a valve to a throat, and on into the hold, a great domed stomach, with egg rooms and birthing halls off it. The spoke deck curves round its south side, lancers wait there, and the summit deck curves round its north side, with a chained docking bore up to a raised cradle. Its objectives: cap the four wellheads, kill the sand crawler, a tracked tanker hauling the last load along the dunes, force the pump station door, destroy its two pump cores, then follow the pipeline to the gate. A reinforced wall in a sandstone butte hides a cache with a launcher upgrade.
18. **SIGMA-4 Refinery Terraces**: a refinery cut into a sandstone hill. A winding gorge opens onto curved terraces that run round a spur of rock like contour lines, past a sunken slag pool, round stacks and domed tanks. #### Episode III: Beyond the Void

The hidden worlds inside the shroud, ending on the Void Herald.

25. **THE SHROUD Dust Veil**: open space twice the usual size at the edge of the Void, so thick with dust that the fog closes in fast, strewn with nearly two hundred rocks, some of them tumbling. 32. **VOID-4 Trophy Field**: open space twice the usual size, where a mismatched armada lies dead: thirteen derelicts from other worlds, every one from a different builder, many of them dwarfing the husks. Gutted lancers with bare frames and broken spines, plated saucers with a sector torn away, container freighters shedding their loads, a cruiser broken in two, sail ships with torn light-sails and a spider-legged hulk drift round a great rounded hulk with a domed hold inside it and the wreck of its bridge on its back. 34. **VOID-5 Ash Mesas**: a dead plain running on to the horizon under a red sky, with stepped basalt mesas at a dozen heights, two winding lava rivers, a lava gorge down its east side and updraft vents. 35. **VOID-6 Choir Spires**: a basalt plain under a violet sky with pools of void, where eleven round spires stand in a ring round a stepped amphitheatre, rising like organ pipes from south to north. 36. **VOID-6 Heart of the Void**: nothing runs straight here. A wandering laser gauntlet leads into the shattered ring, a broken circular deck round a drop, with floating platforms and an updraft at its centre and wings on either side, one behind a secret panel. A second laser corridor opens into the heart chamber, a vast dome with crystal growing up its walls, and the final fight with the Void Herald. Its objectives: destroy the Herald's three nodes, silence the Void Herald, then take the last gate home.

**Chained gates** block some routes. **Core gates** stay shut until every data core in the sector (glowing teal crystals) is destroyed. Objective targets that are machines (fuel vats and pumps, relay masts, consoles, emitters, scrubbers, racked and sleeping fighters, cryo pods, core samples, crucibles, moulds, valves, keel charges, crane hoists and desalination drums) look like what they are, with their working parts glowing; fuel vats and pumps keep burning after they are destroyed. Each gate has two glowing padlocks chained across its face, and shooting both apart sinks the gate into the floor. On the radar, closed gates show as orange outlines. **Secret panels** look almost like the surrounding wall apart from a faint glowing seam; shoot one a few times and it slides away to reveal a hidden room with supplies. **Reinforced walls** are panels split by glowing orange cracks; cannon bolts bounce off them, but missiles, plasma bursts and the lance rail break them open. Many hide bonus caches of missiles and repairs, among them the ammunition shed (sector 3), off the cryo chamber (sector 4), the reef cache (sector 9), off the crane deck (sector 10), the spoke deck (sector 14), the butte cache (sector 17), the upper terrace (sector 18) and the cantor's vault (sector 35). **Toxic sludge** and **molten slag** damage the ship if you fly within a few units of the surface; slag burns harder. **Water** is harmless. **Updraft vents** are glowing fans in the floor whose rising streaks push the ship upward. **Laser grids** are stacks of red beams across a passage that switch on and off on a timer and flicker just before they switch on; flying into a live beam damages the ship.

Set dressing (crate stacks, consoles, pipe clusters rising to the ceiling, storage tanks and lamp posts) stands along the walls of the bigger rooms in every sector, and blocks the ship like a wall. The levels are built on a grid of 5-unit cells. Each cell has its own floor height, and covered cells have a ceiling with tunnel lights, so the ship must fit between floor and ceiling. Each sector sets its own sky height, so the tall ones stack ledges, shafts and pits far above and below each other. Raised floors can be dressed as container stacks, ice shelves or void platforms. A cell can also have a roof: covered up to its ceiling, solid to its roof and open above it, which makes buildings you can fly over and blocks that hang in mid-air. Some sectors add spinning rings, reactor columns and tumbling cubes that block the ship as they turn. In open space a cell can also have open space below its floor, so whole modules float with room all round them. Steel girders cross many routes at different heights, so you have to fly over, under or between them. Hazard-striped posts with amber beacons mark doorways and pillar corners, and the radar draws the walls around you.

### Bosses

Six sectors have a boss: sectors 8, 12, 13, 24, 28 and 36. A health bar appears at the top of the screen once it sees you, the boss theme takes over the music, and the gate onward stays sealed until it is destroyed in a chain of explosions.

Bosses **blink** to dodge fire. Once its shield is down and a boss is hit, it shrugs off that shot, vanishes in a violet flash and reappears somewhere else in its arena, at least 18 units away and clear of your ship. For a moment after, it is out of phase and shots pass through it. Its blink then needs seven seconds to recharge, four and a half once it is below 40% hull; on Easy it waits longer, and on Hard it blinks sooner. The first blink comes three seconds after it first takes damage. Hull points: the Overseer has 140, the Hive Queen and the brood mother 160, the Sentinel 120, the Carrier 180 and the Herald 300.

- **Spire Overseer** (sector 8): a broad hull that holds the summit hall, shielded by two turret relays that fire bursts at you. It fires five-bolt spreads and, every third volley, a charged beam that trails your ship; below 40% it fires faster and calls in drones.
- **Hive Queen** (sectors 12 and 13, where the Brood Ark's brood mother is one too): a great insect with a swollen, banded egg-laying abdomen, eggs glowing along her flanks, on six jointed legs at the heart of the nest, shielded by four egg sacs that hatch mites while you are near. She sprays fans of slow green acid and lays mites; below 40% she lays broods as well.
- **OMEGA Sentinel** (sector 24): a huge turning crown. While any of its four blue shield pylons stand, shots bounce off it. It fires fans of seven bolts and launches homing missiles, two at a time once its shield is down; below 40% it fires faster and calls in hunters.
- **Void Carrier** (sector 28): a long hull, scarred from the crossing to Earth (one wing sheared short, a gash with its ribs bare), that breaks from its mooring cradle and sweeps across its hangar, firing bolt fans and missile pairs and launching skimmers from its bays every other volley. Below 40% it launches mites instead and fires faster.
- **Void Herald** (sector 36): a great crystal crown shielded by three nodes that circle it and shoot back. It alternates spinning rings of bolts with a charged beam that trails your ship before it fires. With its nodes gone it rises and adds homing missiles, and below a third of its hull it fires faster and calls skimmers.

### Map

Press Tab to show or hide the map of the current sector. Rooms appear as they come into view; walls you have not seen stay hidden. Brighter floors are higher, sludge is green, slag is orange, water is dark blue and vents are amber. Areas you have entered are labelled, closed gates are outlined in orange, data cores you have seen show as teal diamonds (with a count of those left), the exit blinks green (a grey ring while it is locked), and the white arrow is your ship. In a sector with objectives, a yellow arrow bobs over the current one, its remaining targets show as yellow diamonds, the radar points to it in yellow, a yellow light column marks it in the world, and the objective, its progress and its distance show under the area name. The game keeps running while the map is open.

### Enemies

- **Drones** (red) hover until they see you, then hold a middle distance and strafe from side to side, bobbing up and down, while firing single bolts.
- **Hunters** (amber, dart-shaped) are fragile, but once they see you they weave in and ram.
- **Wardens** (violet, large) take eight hits, grind forward swaying from side to side and fire three-bolt spreads.
- **Strikers** (teal, wide-winged) hang back and strafe across your view, launching slow homing missiles. A blinking MISSILE WARNING shows when one is close, and you can shoot incoming missiles down.
- **Sentries** (yellow) are turrets mounted on striped pylons that fire three-shot bursts.
- **Lancers** (crimson needles) perch high and paint you with a red aiming beam that trails your ship for about a second, then fire a fast, heavy bolt down it. Keep moving.
- **Aegis** (blue stars) link to up to three allies with blue beams; linked enemies glow blue and take no damage until the aegis dies.
- **Broods** (yellow bulbs) hatch mites while they see you and burst into four more when destroyed. **Mites** are tiny, fast rammers.
- **Skimmers** (green, flat) circle you at speed and fire quick pairs of bolts.
- **Phantoms** (pale blue, wide-winged) are the Void's own hunters, met only inside the shroud. They stay cloaked as a faint shimmer, invisible on radar and impossible to lock on to, until they fire a three-shot burst, get hit or come within close range.
- **Shield bearers** are wardens, sentries, strikers, lancers and drones that carry a one-way shield: a glowing honeycomb disc held in front of them. It stops your bolts, missiles and the lance's rail from the front, and their own fire passes out through it. They set it down facing you and it stays where it was set, with the ones that fly keeping in cover straight behind it. It holds for 6 to 9 seconds and flickers just before it drops. They also drop it early if you get more than about 65 degrees round its edge or out of sight. Once it drops, for whatever reason, it takes about 30 seconds to recharge before they can set down another, so fly round the side or wait it out. They start appearing in sector 2 and are common from sector 5 on.
- **Miners** (orange boxes) patrol back and forth laying mines. **Mines** drift toward a nearby ship, arm after a second and blow up when you get close; shooting one sets it off, which can catch other enemies.

Drones, hunters, wardens, strikers, skimmers and phantoms jink sideways when you line one up and fire, or when you hit it, kicking out across your view and mostly away from the reticle; they jink less often on Easy and more often on Hard. The first time you meet each of the tougher types, a one-line tip appears; types you have met are remembered in your save. A hostile that spots you wakes the others close to it. The open-world sectors (the moons, cylinders, canyons, open space and the Sunless Sea) put three times as many hostiles in their open-air areas, since there is room there to outfly them; the rooms and buildings inside those sectors keep their usual numbers. From sector 8 on, some wardens fly with two escort drones that hold formation either side of them, on the side facing you, until the warden dies. Big enemies sometimes leave a repair or missile crystal behind. From sector 6 on, some enemies are **elites**: gold-eyed and gold-tinted, with nearly twice the hull, and they fire a quarter faster. They grow more common in later sectors.

### Arsenal

Pick up a green crystal to repair 35% of the hull. A pink crystal adds six seeker missiles (up to 24).

An amber crystal upgrades the neutron cannon one tier. Amber crystals collected at the top tier repair 15% of the hull instead. The menu lists every tier you have found. On the curved sectors, the moons and the cylinders, bolts and the rail fly straight as you see them, so they go where the reticle points instead of following the ground's curve.

### Upgrades

Finishing a sector earns one upgrade point, and the upgrade bay opens before the next briefing. Spend the point on a rank in a power or keep it for later; points spent there are fixed. Sector N is flown with N - 1 points, so a full campaign earns 35 points: the build is a choice through the first two episodes, and the 30 ranks on offer can all be filled late in the third. Picking any sector but the first from the menu opens the same screen as a test loadout, with N - 1 points to assign freely before it begins: sector 22 gets 21. A sector remembers the build it was last entered with. New Game clears it. The bar above the cockpit shows what you own and what is ready, and on a touch screen each power gets its own button.

### Curved rooms

A sector's rooms can be ovals, rings and winding tubes as well as rectangles, with domed ceilings and floors that climb. The grid underneath is still square, but the walls are drawn on the true curve and collisions follow it too, so the ship stops at the wall you can see. Small pillars are drawn round the same way, with their walls split into more sides than the grid would give them, and a sector can stand a sheet of rock or ice higher than its top and cut its route down through it.

A cave can run under natural ground with the hill unbroken above it, opening to the sky as a cutting where the hillside falls away, and a dome can have an outer shell, so it reads as a dome from outside as well as in. The same curves work in open space: the hulls of ships and stations have smooth surfaces and a true rim, with curved rooms inside them.

### Textures

All surfaces use textures painted in JavaScript at load time, with no image files. Each texture has a color layer, a height layer that becomes a normal map, and a glow layer for lights. Wall panels come in many variants, including consoles, vents, sealed doors, pipes, windows, signs, rust, frosted ice, hive resin, basalt with magma cracks, cargo containers, clean tech panels and void crystal. Floors include tread plate, glowing grates, landing pads, cracked foundry plates, sludge, slag, ice, resin, water, vents and void tiles. The WebGL renderer adds perspective-correct mapping, mipmaps, a low sun, sky light, the ship's headlight, and colored light from shots and explosions. Dirt and color drift are laid over every level in world space, painted lights and hot cracks come and go in broad patches (whole banks of them are dark in a sector on reserve power), lava carries a drifting crust, and each material has its own highlight sharpness. On HIGH and MEDIUM the frame goes through a half-float buffer and one tone curve that keeps the hue of bright light, so explosions and glows stay colored instead of clipping to white. Some sectors have weather (rain and lightning, spores, ash, dust that veils the stars), standing fires, and beams of power from objective targets to what they feed, each going out as its target dies; destroyed machines are left as smoking husks. Floors give off fumes where there is air to hold them: smoke and embers over lava, green vapour over sludge, mist lying on water and along ice caves, and steam from the vents. Fires, burning wrecks and explosions throw real smoke that covers what is behind it. Each frame draws only the parts of a level that are in view, nearest first, and works out the sky only where it shows.

## Run in a terminal

```sh
node radix.js
```

Use an interactive terminal at least 60 columns wide. Hold **W/S** or the up/down arrows to pitch, **A/D** or the left/right arrows to turn, **Q/E** to roll, **Space** to accelerate, and **X** to brake. **P** pauses; **Esc** or **Ctrl+C** quits. The browser version uses different keys: **W/A/S/D** strafe, **Up** pitches down, **Down** pitches up, and **X** accelerates.

The ship, stars, and rotating wireframe beacons are projected from 3D coordinates into the terminal. This is a starting point for a larger game.

## Autopilot playtest

`tools/playtest` flies sectors automatically in headless Chromium and saves what it sees. It plans a route through each sector's open space, flies it with the game's own controls, shoots the locks on chained gates in its way and the objective targets, and takes a screenshot at every new area, target, completed objective and the exit. In `god` mode the ship takes no damage, which checks that every objective can be reached and finished by flying. In `combat` mode damage is on and the ship fights back, which gives a rough sense of where a sector is hard. The bot aims perfectly but dodges poorly and ignores laser grids, so read its deaths as places to look at, not proof.

```sh
cd tools/playtest
npm install
node playtest.mjs --sectors 1-3 --modes god,combat
node playtest.mjs --sectors 1-36 --modes god --every 5   # also a picture every 5 seconds of flight
```

Results go to `tools/playtest/out/`: a folder of screenshots and a `report.json` per sector and mode, and a `report.md` with the time and damage for each objective. It needs Node and a Chromium installed by Playwright (or set `CHROME` to a Chrome executable). The game exposes what the autopilot needs on `window.radix` only when the page is opened with `?debug`.
